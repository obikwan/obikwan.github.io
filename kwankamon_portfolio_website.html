Team Leadership · Thai Glico · 2019–2021
The team that didn't know it could think for itself

When I joined Thai Glico as the first Thai marketing lead after a long line of Japanese GMs, I inherited a team of nine. Bright people — all of them. But they'd been raised in a culture of top-down instruction, where the job was to execute, not to think. Most had joined straight out of university and had never worked anywhere else. This was the only way they knew.

My instinct was to challenge them — give them real ownership of tasks, run 1-on-1s to slowly open up the space, bring agencies in to build their analytical skills rather than just telling them what the output should be. A lot of them didn't like it at first. When you've been told what to do for years, being asked to figure it out yourself feels like abandonment, not empowerment.

The lesson I keep coming back to: my natural style works well with experienced people who are ready to be challenged. For junior team members, especially early in a culture shift, they often need clear direction first — a 1-2-3 before they're ready to improvise. I learned to read that, and to switch between modes. By the time I left, a handful of people told me it was the most they'd grown in their careers. That's the metric I actually trust.

Digital Experiential · Thai Glico · November 2020
When the event can't happen, you reinvent what an event is

It was Pocky Day 2020 — November 11th — and COVID had shut down everything. The Siam Square activation we'd run the year before wasn't possible. We had a brand with strong fan culture around it, a campaign mechanic built on shared moments, and no way to put people in a room together.

The solution we landed on was a purchase-gated virtual fan experience: buy Pocky, get a chance to join a live 1-on-1 chat session with MewGulf — at the time, the most popular boy-love duo in Thailand. It sounds simple, but the executional logic underneath it was complex: consumer journey design, purchase verification, digital queuing, live streaming — none of it was in the standard CPG marketing playbook.

The campaign trended #1 on Twitter in Thailand during the live event. Over 10,000 viewers. More importantly, it proved something I've believed for a long time: the constraint isn't the problem. How you respond to the constraint is where the interesting marketing happens.

Stakeholder Partnership · Colgate-Palmolive · 2015
The event that ended up on the 8PM national news

Global Handwashing Day exists every year on October 15th. Every soap and handwash brand in the market runs something on that date. The challenge for Protex in 2015 was the same challenge every brand faces when a category occasion belongs to everyone: how do you own a day that nobody owns?

The answer was HRH Princess Somsavalee. We secured a royal partnership for the Protex Handwashing Day activation — an event that, by virtue of the royal presence, automatically entered a different tier of national significance. Events with royal participation in Thailand have access to 8PM primetime national news coverage as a matter of protocol. That's not a media buy. That's earned reach at a scale no budget in our category could replicate.

The event itself reached 400–500 people on the ground. The national news coverage reached millions. The lesson was about the leverage in stakeholder selection — the right partnership doesn't just add credibility, it changes the category of the thing you're doing.
